The Last Shift at Blackwater Station黑水站的最后一班

0:00 / 0:00

课文

Old Mr. Bao had worked at Blackwater Station for forty years.

老包先生已经在黑水车站干了四十年。

His pension was small, but the welfare was good: a travel allowance, a fee for every night shift, and a share of the profit from the station shop.

他的养老金不多，但福利不错：一份差旅补贴、每上一个夜班有一笔酬金，还能分到车站小卖部的一份利润。

The best aspect of the job, he always said, was not the benefit of the money but the people.

他总说，这份工作最好的一面不是那点好处，而是这些人。

On his last morning a young inspector came to inspect the ticket office.

他最后一个上班日的早上，一位年轻的检查员来检查售票处。

For an hour he watched the platform, as silent as a spy, and he seemed suspicious of everything.

他在站台上看了一个小时，安静得像个间谍，对什么都显得疑神疑鬼。

"I respect your work," he said, "but the numbers do not match."

“我尊重你的工作，”他说，“可这些数字对不上。”

He began to speculate about the missing fare, and he asked to monitor the machine himself.

他开始推测那笔不见了的车费去了哪儿，还要求自己亲手盯着那台机器。

From his perspective, Mr. Bao was a suspect.

在他看来，包先生就是个嫌疑人。

The two clerks handed in their respective reports.

两个售票员各自交上了自己的报表。

Mr. Bao did not argue.

包先生没有争辩。

"Look at the prospect," he said.

“看看前景吧，”他说。

"Forty years, and not one passenger cheated us."

“四十年了，没有一个乘客骗过我们。”

The lower half of the station lay in a hollow between two hills, where the wind swallowed the sound of the trains.

车站的下半截建在两座山之间的一个洼地里，冬天风一吹，火车的声音就被吞掉了。

In his office there was an old pillow on a bench and a telescope pointed at the horizon.

他的办公室里有一条长凳，上面放着一只旧枕头，还有一架望远镜对着地平线。

On clear days he could see the boundary of the next county.

天气好的时候，他能看见邻县的地界。

At noon the spectators gathered for his farewell.

中午，观众们聚在站台上，为他送别。

Someone had hung a conspicuous red flag above the roof, and the margin of the platform was crowded with friends.

有人在屋顶上方挂了一面显眼的红旗，站台的边缘挤满了朋友。

The inspector found his mistake before the flag came down.

旗还没降下来，检查员就发现自己算错了。

"I hope the station prospers," Mr. Bao said, and he waved goodbye to the crowd.

“我希望这个车站越来越兴旺，”包先生说完，朝人群挥手告别。

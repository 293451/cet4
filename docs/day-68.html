The Kettle by the Wayside路边的水壶

0:00 / 0:00

课文

Forty years ago, Nora's father paved the last kilometer of the mountain road himself.

四十年前，诺拉的爸爸一个人把这条山路的最后一千米铺成了路。

At the wayside, where the road turns, he built a small tea house with a tin roof.

在路边那个拐弯的地方，他盖了一间铁皮顶的小茶馆。

When he died, Nora took over the tea house, although she had no training in leadership.

他去世后，诺拉接手了茶馆，尽管她从没受过任何领导力的训练。

All she had was her father's notebook, which she used as a guide.

她手上只有爸爸的一本旧笔记，她把它当成自己的指南。

Inside were his guidelines: boil the water, wipe the cups, and never sit down before the last guest leaves.

里面是他写下的准则：把水烧开，把杯子擦干净，最后一位客人走之前绝不坐下。

Her uncle tried to mislead her from the first week.

她叔叔从第一周起就想把她带偏。

"Sell liquor and beer," he said.

“卖烈性酒和啤酒，”他说。

"Add alcohol and cigarettes, and the tourists will stay all night."

“再加点酒精和香烟，游客就会待一整晚。”

Some neighbours misunderstood her refusal, and their misunderstanding hurt her more than the money.

有些邻居误解了她的拒绝，而这份误解比钱更让她难受。

Then a storm came and the tin roof fell in, a real misfortune for a woman of thirty.

后来一场暴风雨掀翻了铁皮屋顶，对一个三十岁的女人来说，这是真正的不幸。

For a whole month, only a kettle, a basin and a sheet of nylon kept the place alive.

整整一个月，只有一只水壶、一个盆和一块尼龙布撑着这个店。

Nora did not give up; she began to measure things instead.

诺拉没有放弃，她转而开始测量。

She wrote down the temperature of the water in degrees and drew a chart on the back of an old calendar.

她把水温记成多少度，还在旧日历的背面画了一张图表。

She measured the clouds in centimeters every morning.

她每天早上用厘米量云。

She put her teas on a kitchen scale.

她把茶叶放上厨房的秤。

Then she ranked them from one to ten and wrote the list on the wall.

然后她把它们从一到十分出等级，把单子写在墙上。

On average, only three cars stopped each day.

平均每天只有三辆车停下来。

But tea with a magnetic smell and a warm welcome became common, then commonplace.

可是带着磁性香气的茶，加上一声温暖的问候，渐渐变得常见，再变得平凡。

Old customers called her tea a miracle and said she worked magic with leaves and water.

老主顾说她的茶是奇迹，说她用叶子和水变魔法。

Nora only laughed: "It is just a beverage."

诺拉只是笑：“不过是一杯饮料罢了。”

Nobody mentioned the drunk men from the bar down the road.

没人提过路那头酒吧里那些喝醉的男人。

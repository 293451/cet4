The Soap Seller and the Town Statute卖肥皂的年轻人与小镇法规

0:00 / 0:00

课文

Wei was a naive young man with an innocent face, and half the market trusted him at once.

韦是个天真的年轻人，长着一张无辜的脸，半个市场的人立刻就信了他。

He was sure that every merchant was decent, and that nobody would cheat a beginner.

他确信每个商人都正派，没人会骗一个刚入行的人。

On the first of May he rented a small stall and began to sell soap and candles.

五月一日，他租下一个小摊位，开始卖肥皂和蜡烛。

The town had strict legislation about trade, and the legal side of a small shop frightened him.

小镇对买卖有严格的法规，小店的合法问题让他有点发怵。

He was responsible for every item on his own paper.

纸上每一项都得由他自己负责。

"A statute is a statute," the clerk said.

“法规就是法规，”办事员说。

"Your paper is not valid until we validate it, and the tax office will execute the rule."

“你的材料在我们核实之前不算有效，税务所会执行这条规定。”

The law obliged him to pay on time, and he signed.

法律迫使他按时缴税，他签了字。

Six months later a soap crisis hit the market and prices doubled.

半年后，一场肥皂危机席卷市场，价格翻了一倍。

Soap was a common commodity, yet that month it felt like gold.

肥皂本是寻常商品，可那个月它却像黄金一样珍贵。

Wei had no finance and little stock, and he found no loophole in the contract.

韦没有资金，存货也很少，合同里也找不到任何漏洞。

So he went to an executive at the town's commerce office.

于是他去找镇上商业办公室的一位主管帮忙。

She offered him a simple incentive.

她给了他一个简单的激励。

"Publish one short notice," she said.

“印一份简短的通知，”她说。

"Release your low price to the public, and the customers will come."

“把你的低价告诉公众，顾客自然会上门。”

He did, and they came.

他照做了，顾客真的来了。

By autumn his merchandise was gone in two days, and other merchants brought their stock.

到了秋天，他的货两天就卖光了，其他商人也把存货送到他这儿。

Trade in the street grew, and people began to call him clever.

街上的买卖越做越大，人们开始说他机灵。

He drew a small sun on every box, and that sun is his trademark today.

他在每个盒子上画了一个小太阳，那个太阳就是他今天的商标。

Each evening he wrote his budget and his target on the wall.

每晚他都在墙上写下自己的预算和目标。

Above the door a painter had painted the word revenue as revent, and Wei never changed it.

门上方，油漆匠曾把 revenue 写成了 revent，韦一直没让人改。

His shop is still called Revent Soap.

他的店至今还叫“Revent 肥皂店”。

The Glassmaker制玻璃的人

课文

The fire must ignite before anything else can happen.

火必须先点燃，别的才谈得上。

He would highlight the hottest point with a thin iron rod, and the solar heat from the sunlight at sunset was enough for the first melt.

他会用一根细铁棍标出最热的那个点，而日落时阳光带来的热量就够第一次熔化了。

Long before dawn he was already at work.

天还没亮他就已经在干活了。

A solid base had to consolidate before the fluid would hold its shape, and the liquid glass turned like slow honey.

得先形成一个坚实的底，流体才能定住形状，液态玻璃转动起来像缓慢的蜂蜜。

The volume of the furnace mattered, and there was a minimum and a maximum for every batch.

炉子的容积很要紧，每一炉都有个最小量和最大量。

He lowered the vessel into the heat.

他把容器放低，送进热里。

He could feel the pulse of the flame, and a sudden impulse made him pull it back.

他能感到火焰的搏动，一个突然的冲动让他把它抽了回来。

A flood of orange light seemed to fluctuate across the floor.

一片橘色的光在地面上起伏闪动。

He had to bleed the air out slowly, then dry the surface before it cooled.

他得慢慢地把空气放掉，然后在它冷却之前把表面烘干。

The glass would bubble, and one bulb of air could ruin the whole piece.

玻璃会起泡，一个气泡就能毁掉整件作品。

His work was a kind of gesture, a mortgage on his own patience.

他的活计是某种姿态，是拿自己的耐心作抵押。

He tried to engage the material on its own terms.

他试着按照材料本身的性子去和它打交道。

It was a cognitive task as much as a physical one, and he could recognize a good piece at a glance.

这既是认知的活，也是体力的活；他一眼就能认出一件好东西。

The real challenge was never the glass, but the waiting.

真正的难关从来不是玻璃，而是等待。

He planted a small flag on the shelf and set a global rule for himself.

他在架子上插了一面小旗，给自己定了一条通用的规矩。

The Night the Power Went Out停电的那一夜

0:00 / 0:00

课文

Old Chen ran a small business at the end of Market Lane: he sold cloth by the metre.

老陈在市场巷的尽头做着一门小生意：他按米卖布。

That Friday a typhoon hit the coast, and at eight the power went out.

那个星期五，一场台风袭击了海岸，八点停电了。

The shop had no backup light, and in the dark a shelf fell down.

店里没有备用的灯，黑暗中一个货架倒了下来。

The whole shop became a mess; boxes lay in disorder and the street was in chaos.

整个店变成一团糟；箱子乱七八糟，外面的街上一片混乱。

Above the door, a hand-painted sign still read srtipe.

门上方，一块手写的招牌上还写着 srtipe。

Chen's father had painted a wrong letter there years before, and Chen never had the heart to change it.

那是陈的父亲多年前写错的一个字母，陈一直不忍心去改。

Chen had strong willpower.

陈的意志力很强。

He was willing to work all night, though his wife was reluctant to leave the house.

他愿意干一整夜，尽管妻子不愿离开家。

He did not restrain her or restrict her words.

他没有去制止她，也没有限制她说话。

He only asked her to constrain the damage and keep the water out.

他只请她尽量控制住损失，把水挡在门外。

The heavy rain strained the old roof and the door.

大雨把老屋顶和门都绷得紧紧的。

Chen grabbed the rolling door and tried to grasp the handle.

陈抓住卷帘门，想握住门把手。

The wind could rip a sign off the wall.

风能把招牌从墙上撕下来。

A strip of cloth flew past, and he caught it with a firm grip.

一条布带飞过，他一把抓住，握得很紧。

The rising water could trap anyone in the low lane.

上涨的水会把低处巷子里的任何人困住。

By midnight the neighbours came with buckets and lamps.

到了半夜，邻居们带着水桶和灯来了。

To be fair, everyone did an equal share of the work.

说句公道话，每个人都出了一份同等的力。

Chen tried to coordinate the buckets and the lamps.

陈想办法协调水桶和灯的使用。

He did not equate his own effort with theirs.

他没有把自己的付出和别人的等同起来。

There was not adequate light, so they worked fairly slowly.

灯光不够，所以他们干得相当慢。

One lamp was the equivalent of ten candles.

一盏灯相当于十根蜡烛。

By morning the water had gone, and orderly rows of cloth were back on the shelf.

到早上，水退了，一排排整齐的布重新摆上了货架。

Chen wrote the whole affair down in his notebook.

陈把这整件事记在了笔记本上。

He decided to abolish the old rule that only family could run the shop.

他决定废除那条“只有家里人才能看店”的旧规矩。

From that day, anyone who was willing to help was welcome.

从那天起，任何愿意帮忙的人都受欢迎。

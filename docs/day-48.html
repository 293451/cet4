The Shop on Cedar Street雪松街上的铺子

0:00 / 0:00

课文

Cedar Street had one last shop, where Mrs. Han restored old clocks.

雪松街上还剩最后一家铺子，韩太太在那里修老钟。

The clocks were delicate, and so was she.

那些钟很精致，她本人也一样，碰不得。

Last winter she fell on the ice and could not stand.

去年冬天她在冰上摔了一跤，站都站不起来。

The doctor injected a remedy into the joint; it would take months to heal.

医生往她的关节里打了一剂药；要几个月才能好。

"You will recover," he said, "but slowly."

“你会康复的，”他说，“但是很慢。”

She did not want to rest.

她不想休息。

For fifty years she had devoted herself to broken clocks.

五十年来，她把自己整个交给了那些坏钟。

She had dedicated her life to them, and now they owe her something.

她把一生都献给了它们，现在轮到它们欠她点什么了。

Her neighbours rejected the idea of closing the shop, and nobody objected.

邻居们不接受关店这个主意，也没有人反对留下来。

They donated money, contributed tools, and distributed a notice along the street.

他们捐了钱，出了工具，还沿街发了一张通知。

They bundled up their broken things, a kettle, an antique vase, and carried them to her door.

他们把自己家坏掉的东西——一只水壶、一只古董花瓶——捆起来，搬到了她门口。

"The old word for this is retribution," they said. "A giving back."

“这事儿的老说法叫报应，”他们说，“是一种还回去。”

A student named Tao came with a project: to photograph old shops.

一个叫陶的学生带着一个计划来了：拍老街上的铺子。

He anticipated a refusal, but she agreed to participate.

他预料会被拒绝，可她却答应参加。

"I have one subject," he said. "You."

“我只有一个拍摄对象，”他说，“就是您。”

"I do not object to a camera," she said. "Only to pity."

“我不反对相机，”她说，“我只反对可怜。”

His old camera ejected each photo like a flat bird.

他那台旧相机吐出照片的样子，像一只只扁平的鸟。

By spring, one quiet phenomenon was easy to see: a busy shop never falls apart.

到了春天，有一个安静的现象一眼就能看见：一家热闹的铺子，是不会散掉的。

Tao's photos combined the street's stories into one wall of faces.

陶的照片把这条街的故事拼成了一整面人脸墙。

In conjunction with three other shops, he hung them next door, though the printer set conjunction as conjunctjon.

他和另外三家铺子一起把照片挂在了隔壁，只是印厂把 conjunction 印成了 conjunctjon。

The street, which had never had any cohesion, began to connect.

这条从来没有过什么凝聚力的街，开始连了起来。

Now the city plans to integrate these small shops into the new district.

现在市里打算把这些小店并进新街区。

Mrs. Han attributed her recovery to the street, not to the doctor.

韩太太把她的康复归功于这条街，而不是医生。

Her knee healed, and the street was restored, not by medicine, but by its own people.

她的膝盖好了，这条街也修好了——不是靠药，是靠它自己的人。
